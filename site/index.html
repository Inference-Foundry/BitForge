1. Mathematical Foundations & Granularity

This section establishes the formal mathematical definitions of tensor quantization, distinguishing between affine mappings and the structural implications of group-wise granularity.

1.1 The Core Math: Affine vs. Symmetric

Quantization maps high-precision floating-point values $x \in \mathbb{R}$ to lower-precision integer representations $x_q \in \mathbb{Z}$ via a scaling factor $s$ and a zero-point $z$.

Uniform Affine (Asymmetric) Quantization

Maps the min/max of the float range to the min/max of the integer range. Ideal for skewed distributions (e.g., ReLU activations).

$$ s = \frac{x_{max} - x_{min}}{q_{max} - q_{min}} $$ $$ z = \lfloor q_{min} - \frac{x_{min}}{s} \rceil $$ $$ x_q = \text{clip}\left(\lfloor \frac{x}{s} \rceil + z, q_{min}, q_{max}\right) $$

Where $\lfloor \cdot \rceil$ denotes the Round-to-Nearest (RTN) function, and $\text{clip}(v, a, b) = \max(a, \min(v, b))$. Dequantization reconstructs the approximate float: $\tilde{x} = s(x_q - z)$.

Symmetric Quantization

Forces the zero-point $z=0$, simplifying vector dot-product hardware. The float range is made symmetric around zero before mapping.

$$ s = \frac{\max(|x_{max}|, |x_{min}|)}{q_{max}} $$ $$ x_q = \text{clip}\left(\lfloor \frac{x}{s} \rceil, -q_{max}, q_{max}\right) $$

1.2 Granularity Trade-offs

Per-Tensor

One scale $s$ and zero-point $z$ for the entire weight matrix. High hardware efficiency, extreme quantization error due to outlier domination.

Per-Channel

One scalar per output channel (row/col). Standard for INT8 PTQ. Balances computational error with minimal memory overhead for scaling factors.

Per-Group (Block-wise)

Divides channels into local groups (e.g., $g=128$). Crucial for sub-4-bit models. Isolates dynamic spatial outliers but increases scales overhead.

1.3 The Weight vs. Activation Asymmetry

Quantizing weights alone (e.g., W4A16) is purely a memory-bandwidth optimization. However, W8A8 or W4A8 introduces catastrophic difficulty due to the mathematical origin of dynamic range spikes in LLM activations.

Empirically, $>6$B parameter models exhibit systemic, massive outliers in specific activation channels. While weight distributions $W \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2)$ are tight, activation distributions $X$ have channels where $X_{i,j} > 100 \times \mathbb{E}[X]$.

If per-tensor or naive per-token quantization is applied to $X$, these outliers force the scale factor $s$ to be massive. Consequently, 99.9% of normal activation values are crushed to zero by the RTN function $\lfloor \frac{x}{s} \rceil = 0$, completely destroying the attention mechanism's representational capacity.

2. Architectural Breakthroughs

Deconstructing the exact optimization objectives and algorithms driving modern Post-Training Quantization (PTQ) and local execution.

GPTQ: Generalized Post-Training Quantization

Layer-wise Optimal Brain Surgeon (OBS) Optimization

GPTQ solves the layer-wise weight quantization problem by minimizing the squared error of the output activations for a given calibration set $X$:

$$ \arg\min_{\hat{W}} ||WX - \hat{W}X||_2^2 $$

Key Technical Principles:

  • Inverse Hessian Correction: It utilizes the inverse Hessian matrix $H^{-1} = (2XX^T)^{-1}$ to gauge the sensitivity of weights. If a weight is quantized and an error $\delta$ is introduced, GPTQ updates the remaining unquantized weights to compensate.
  • Weight Update Rule: The optimal compensation for the remaining weights $W_{remain}$ given an error $\delta_{q}$ at weight index $q$ is:
    $\Delta W_{remain} = - \frac{\delta_q}{[H^{-1}]_{qq}} \cdot H^{-1}_{q,:}$
  • Lazy Batch Updates: OBS is strictly sequential ($O(d_{col}^3)$). GPTQ achieves scalability via lazy batch updates. It quantizes weights in blocks (e.g., 128 columns) and applies the Cholesky decomposition to update the remaining matrix in dense matrix-matrix multiplications, converting the process to memory-bound rather than compute-bound during calibration.

AWQ: Activation-Aware Weight Quantization

Protecting 1% of Salient Weights

AWQ posits that weights are not equally important. Keeping just ~1% of salient weights in FP16/BF16 significantly reduces quantization error. Crucially, weight salience is dictated by activation magnitudes, not weight magnitudes.

Activation Scaling

By reviewing calibration activations $X$, AWQ observes that channels with massive averages correspond directly to columns in the weight matrix that hold vital logical mappings.

The Dynamic Scaling Hack

To bypass slow mixed-precision runtimes, AWQ scales up salient weight channels by $s$ and scales down activation channels by $1/s$. The resulting math remains equivalent while weights are safely compressed to INT4.

Optimization Objective:

Find optimal per-channel scaling factors $s$ to minimize quantization error without updating the actual weight values (unlike GPTQ):

$$ L(s) = || WX - Q(W \cdot \Delta_s) \cdot \Delta_s^{-1}X || $$ $$ \text{where } \Delta_s = \text{diag}(s) $$

SmoothQuant

Migrating Difficulty: W8A8 Enabler

SmoothQuant addresses the W8A8 problem directly. Since activations are too hard to quantize due to massive outliers, and weights are easy to quantize, SmoothQuant shifts the magnitude variance from activations to weights mathematically before quantization.

The Mathematical Transformation:

Given linear layer $Y = WX$, we introduce an invertible diagonal scaling matrix $\text{diag}(s)$:

$$ Y = (W \cdot \text{diag}(s)^{-1}) \cdot (\text{diag}(s) \cdot X) $$ $$ \hat{W} = W \cdot \text{diag}(s)^{-1} \quad ; \quad \hat{X} = \text{diag}(s) \cdot X $$

The scaling factor $s_i$ for the $i$-th channel balances the maximum magnitudes of weights and activations, controlled by migration strength parameter $\alpha$ (typically 0.5):

$$ s_i = \frac{\max(|X_i|)^\alpha}{\max(|W_i|)^{1-\alpha}} $$

This "smooths" the activation outliers by suppressing them, pushing the variance into the weight matrix $\hat{W}$, which is then safely quantized to INT8 using standard per-channel methods.

bitsandbytes (LLM.int8() & NF4)

Vector-wise Isolation and Information-Density

LLM.int8() Methodology

Uses vector-wise quantization combined with outlier isolation. It dynamically identifies activation outliers ($> \tau$, usually 6.0) during runtime. The matrix multiplication is split: outlier columns (usually ~0.1%) are computed in FP16, while the remaining 99.9% are multiplied in INT8. Results are summed. This guarantees zero degradation but incurs runtime branching overhead.

NormalFloat4 (NF4) for QLoRA

NF4 is theoretically optimal for zero-mean normal distributions. Instead of evenly spacing bins (like standard INT4), NF4 spaces the quantization bins such that each bin contains exactly the same probability mass (area under the Gaussian curve).

Quantile Quantization Formula:

$$ q_i = \Phi^{-1}\left(\frac{i}{2^k + 1}\right) $$

Where $\Phi^{-1}$ is the quantile function of the standard normal distribution $\mathcal{N}(0,1)$, and $k=4$ for 4-bit. This ensures maximum information density per bit for pre-trained neural network weights.

3. Runtime Engines & The Inference Boundary

Contrasting the execution mechanics of local offloading formats versus high-throughput server runtimes, and the fundamental shift in hardware bottlenecks.

GGUF (llama.cpp)

CPU/GPU Heterogeneous Offloading

  • Memory Layout: Specifically designed for mmap() sequential reads. Embeds quantization metadata directly alongside tensor blocks.
  • Execution Mechanics: Optimized for batch size 1 (interactive generation). It loads block-wise INT4 weights into CPU caches or GPU VRAM, dequantizes them in FP32/FP16 registers using SIMD instructions, and multiplies with FP32/FP16 activations.
  • Optimization Focus: Overcoming severe memory bandwidth constraints on consumer system architectures (DDR5 / unified memory boundaries).

vLLM / TensorRT-LLM

High-Throughput Server Runtimes

  • KV-Cache Management: Utilizes PagedAttention to eliminate memory fragmentation. The KV-cache often remains unquantized (FP16) or lightly quantized (INT8/FP8) to preserve generation fidelity in large contexts.
  • Execution Mechanics: Uses custom W4A16 or W8A8 fused CUDA kernels (e.g., Marlin, AWQ kernels). Optimized for massive batched throughput.
  • Optimization Focus: Maximizing Compute utilization (TFLOPS) while managing KV-cache memory capacity walls in multi-user environments.

Kernel Bottlenecks: The Roofline Shift

In standard FP16 LLM generation (batch size 1), the system is strictly memory-bandwidth bound. The compute units sit idle waiting for weights to load. Mixed-precision runtimes (W4A16) alter this model. By compressing weights to 4-bit, we load data 4x faster, moving the application up the y-axis of the roofline model toward the compute-bound ceiling. However, the runtime overhead of dequantizing those 4-bit weights back to FP16 inside the GPU registers adds new arithmetic intensity, creating a unique sub-bottleneck where integer decoding instructions can saturate before FMA (Fused Multiply-Add) limits.

4. Empirical Evaluation & Calibration

Analyzing the degradation profiles across different quantization depths and the non-trivial impact of calibration datasets.

Degradation Profiling: Reasoning vs. Recall

Empirical literature indicates that quantization impacts model capabilities asymmetrically. Absolute factual recall (e.g., TriviaQA) degrades linearly and gracefully down to INT3. However, abstract reasoning and Chain-of-Thought (CoT) capabilities (e.g., GSM8k, Math) face a catastrophic "capability cliff" transitioning from INT4 to INT3/INT2, as compressed weight spaces fail to retain the complex multi-dimensional manifolds required for logical routing.

Calibration Dynamics (PTQ)

Post-Training Quantization relies on a small dataset (e.g., 128-512 sequences) to calculate clipping thresholds and inverse Hessians. The statistical nature of this data permanently biases the quantized model.

Sequence Length

Calibration on short sequences (512 tokens) truncates the activation space. The model fails to capture RoPE attention outlier spikes that only emerge at >4k contexts, leading to exploding perplexity on long-context tasks post-quantization.

Domain Skew

Using WikiText for GPTQ calibration destructively impacts coding capabilities. Code generates vastly different activation outliers than natural language. Multi-domain mixture is strictly required for generalist preservation.

Distribution Outliers

Algorithms using Min-Max scaling are severely compromised if the calibration set contains anomalous text producing extreme, rare activation spikes, artificially widening the dynamic range and increasing RTN noise for average tokens.

5. Open Research Questions (BitForge Mandate)

Identify structural friction points in the 2026 quantization landscape that represent high-value targets for systematic investigation.

Current INT2 approaches heavily rely on block-wise group sizes (e.g., g=32) where the scale parameter overhead effectively makes the model 2.6-bit. Research vector: Can we develop ternary (-1, 0, 1) or completely non-linear dense codebooks (like VQ) that map sub-2-bit weights to dynamic Look-Up Tables (LUTs) in L1 cache, entirely bypassing standard ALUs for dot products?

As context windows expand beyond 1 Million tokens, the KV-cache exceeds weight memory. Quantizing the KV-cache to INT4/FP8 introduces compounding error over recurrent attention layers. Research vector: Formulate a theoretical boundary for "quantization drift" in auto-regressive attention mechanisms, and design token-eviction algorithms aware of quantization sensitivity.

Standard LLM quantization assumes dense activation patterns. In MoE architectures (like Mixtral/Grok), activation distributions change radically depending on the chosen expert router path per token. Research vector: Develop expert-aware calibration methodologies where $s$ and $z$ dynamically shift based on the preceding routing probabilities, mitigating catastrophic dropouts in niche expert layers.

Post-Training Quantization (PTQ) is cheap, but Quantization-Aware Training (QAT) yields superior results by updating weights using Straight-Through Estimators (STE). Research vector: Investigate why QAT on RLHF/Instruct-tuned models often causes severe behavioral collapse (loss of refusal mechanisms or formatting collapse) compared to applying PTQ to the same model, and establish mathematically robust QAT regularization for alignment preservation.
